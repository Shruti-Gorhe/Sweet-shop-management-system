RPG Vendor Leverage Engine
 
Edit
The RPG Vendor Leverage Engine is a proposed system designed to identify overlapping vendors and contract categories across subsidiaries within the RPG group. By consolidating this information, the engine enables group-level negotiations for commonly purchased goods and services, unlocking immediate cost savings without requiring significant changes to existing processes or technologies. • Addresses inefficiencies from subsidiaries independently negotiating with the same vendors at different rates. • Facilitates consolidated negotiations by providing visibility into shared vendor relationships and contract categories. • Delivers immediate, low-risk savings across multiple subsidiaries' profit and loss statements. • Requires minimal stakeholder behavior change and no new technology implementation. • Initial feasibility is high, with a pilot planned to match vendor data between two subsidiaries. Problem AddressedSubsidiaries within the RPG group currently negotiate with the same vendors independently, often resulting in inconsistent rates and missed opportunities for group-level cost savings. The lack of shared visibility into vendor relationships and contract categories prevents the group from leveraging its collective purchasing power. Proposed SolutionThe proposal is to develop a matching agent that analyzes vendor master data and contract categories across subsidiaries. By identifying overlaps, the engine enables the group to negotiate collectively for common inputs, achieving better terms and pricing. The approach does not require changes to existing procurement processes or new technology adoption by most stakeholders. Expected ImpactThe engine is expected to generate immediate and low-risk cost savings across the group by consolidating negotiations. These savings will directly benefit the profit and loss statements of multiple subsidiaries, enhancing overall group efficiency and competitiveness. Feasibility and RisksThe main technical challenge is normalizing contract categories across different subsidiaries to ensure accurate matching. The initial pilot will focus on matching vendor master data between two subsidiaries to validate the approach. Key dependencies include obtaining access to vendor and contract category data (excluding pricing) from participating subsidiaries. Successful category normalization is critical to the project's effectiveness.

Project Info
Description
One negotiation, at group volume, landing in every subsidiary's P&L at once. Team Lead: Saipriyadarshan Cindula, Sr. GenAI Architect, Zensar Technologies (ZenLabs AI Practice). This submission is made by, and this agent would be built and operated by, Zensar Technologies (ZenLabs AI Practice) on behalf of the RPG group; Zensar Technologies is the submitting company of record for this proposal. The Problem, In Our Words: CEAT, KEC, Raychem RPG, and RPG Life Sciences each negotiate common-category contracts, packaging, steel, logistics, independently, with no shared visibility. It's common, in a group this size, for two or three subsidiaries to be buying the exact same input from the exact same vendor at different rates, and for nobody to know it. What We're Proposing: a matching agent, naturally running on the RPG NeuralMesh knowledge fabric already proposed in this submission set, that cross-references each subsidiary's vendor and contract data, without exposing anyone's actual pricing to another subsidiary, and flags overlapping vendors and categories directly to Group Procurement. The cascade: the agent flags that CEAT, KEC, and Raychem RPG all buy packaging materials from the same vendor, at three different rates. Group Procurement opens one renegotiation at the vendor, at combined group volume, using the best of the three existing rates as the opening ask. The renegotiated rate is applied across all three subsidiaries simultaneously, one conversation, three contracts updated. The saving lands in three P&Ls at once, created by a single negotiation that needed almost no new technology, just visibility that didn't exist before. Why this fits the Innovation Festival: it's the fastest, lowest-risk saving in this entire set, no process changes, no new equipment, no behavior change required from anyone except Group Procurement having one more conversation than they otherwise would have. How it works: a matching agent runs over each subsidiary's vendor master and contract data, with category-normalization as the only real technical challenge, matching "packaging" across subsidiaries that each name it slightly differently. The output is a flagged-overlap report to Group Procurement, the agent surfaces the opportunity, a human runs the actual negotiation. MVP / Phase 1 (2-3 weeks): run the matching pass against just two subsidiaries' vendor master data to validate the category-normalization approach before scaling group-wide. The Ask: vendor master/contract category data, not pricing detail if that's more comfortable for a first pass, from two subsidiaries to run the pilot match.
